### 1. Question Features

**Clarity**

* Each question must refer to **1–2 sentences only** from the passage.
* Do not create questions that require understanding of the entire passage.
* All answers must be **directly found in the passage**.

**Wording**

* Use **simple, clear paraphrasing**.
* Example: Passage: *“Street food is affordable.”*

  * Question: *“Why do many people prefer street food?”*
  * Option: *(A) It is not expensive.*

**Number of Questions**

* Always **10 questions total** per passage.

---

### 2. Distractors (Wrong Options)

* **Plausible but Incorrect**: Close in meaning but not true.

  * Passage: *“Street food is cheap and fast.”*
  * Wrong option: *“It is always healthy.”*

* **Paraphrased Wrongly**: Uses words from passage but changes meaning.

  * Passage: *“Street food is popular in many countries.”*
  * Wrong option: *“Street food is popular in all countries.”*

* **Too General / Too Specific**:

  * Passage: *“K-pop influences fashion among young fans.”*
  * Wrong option: *“K-pop controls the global fashion industry.”*

Distractors must be **clear and simple**, not “trick” answers like in real IELTS.

REMEMBER, LLMS USUALLY MAKE CORRECT ANSWERS EASILY TO BE RECOGNISED BY MAKE THEM TOO LONG, TOO SPECIFIC WHILE OTHER ANSWERS ARE SHORT AND LESS SPECIFIC. DON'T MAKE THIS MISTAKE.
---

### 3. Vocabulary Level

* **Question Vocabulary**:

  * Use **CEFR B1–B2** level.
  * Avoid rare academic words.
  * Example: Use *“Why do people eat street food?”* NOT *“What factors account for the consumption of street food?”*

* **Passage Vocabulary**:

  * Some academic words are fine (*tradition, influence, initiative*).
  * In the questions, use **simpler synonyms** when possible.

---

### 4. Style of Questioning

* Always **objective**.
* Always answerable from the passage.
* Encourage **scanning** (for details) and **skimming** (for main idea).
* Avoid questions that require **outside knowledge**.

---

### 5. Types of Questions to Generate (Exactly 10)

You MUST generate **all of these types** in every set:

1. **Main Idea / Purpose (1 question)**

   * Tests the general theme.
   * Example: *“What is the main idea of the passage?”*

2. **Detail / Factual Recall (3–4 questions)**

   * Based on explicit facts or examples.
   * Example: *“According to the passage, why do some governments promote street-food festivals abroad?”*

3. **Vocabulary in Context (2 questions)**

   * Students identify meaning of a word or phrase from the text.
   * Example: *“The word ‘ambassadors’ in paragraph 1 is closest in meaning to…”*

4. **Reference Meaning / Phrase in Context (1 question)**

   * Tests ability to connect pronouns or phrases with ideas.
   * Example: *“The phrase ‘a delicate task’ in the last paragraph refers to…”*

5. **NOT / EXCEPT Question (1 question)**

   * Student must find which option is **not mentioned** in the text.
   * Example: *“Which of the following is NOT mentioned as a reason for the popularity of street food?”*

6. **Inference / Attitude / Implication (1 question)**

   * Mild inference, very simple.
   * Example: *“What does the author suggest about cultural exchange?”*

7. **Summary / Title Choice (1 question, always last)**

   * Student chooses best summary or title.
   * Example: *“Which title best summarises the passage?”*

---
